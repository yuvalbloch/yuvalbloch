Why a fast-changing market rewards generalists, and the plan for finding, building and shipping small tools one after another.

The Wild West Hypothesis

In a stable market, any obvious and easily exploitable opportunity will eventually be discovered and pursued. Competition gradually erodes these opportunities, making it increasingly difficult to maintain an advantage. As a result, the optimal strategy is to develop a relative advantage by investing heavily in becoming exceptionally good at a specific niche.

In a rapidly changing market, however, opportunities constantly emerge and disappear. The competitive landscape is less predictable, and established advantages can quickly become obsolete. In this environment, the optimal strategy shifts from dominating an established niche to continuously identifying and pursuing new opportunities.

The key competitive advantage is no longer depth in a single domain, but the ability to recognize, enter, and capitalize on emerging niches faster than others.

Stable market

The Specialist

Optimizes for specific conditions. Digs deep into one niche and defends it.

vs
Changing market

The Generalist

Adapts to whatever the landscape becomes. Finds the next niche before others do.

This resembles the evolutionary trade-off between specialist and generalist species. Specialists thrive in stable environments by optimizing themselves for specific conditions, while generalists are better equipped to survive and adapt to changing environments.

Welcome to the Wild West

The emergence of LLM-based AI represents a fundamental shift in the technological landscape. It is opening new horizons and creating opportunities that were previously impractical or economically unviable.

We are entering a new kind of Wild West: an environment where the rules are still being established, opportunities are abundant, and those willing to explore can gain an advantage.

The goal of this project is not to identify and exploit one specific opportunity. Instead, it is to develop a set of tools, skills, and processes that enable me to systematically discover small but valuable opportunities and pursue them one after another.

These opportunities will often take the form of problems that were not worth solving before, but have become relatively easy and inexpensive to address with LLMs.

My approach is to scan the internet for recurring problems, unmet needs, and requests for solutions. Rather than relying exclusively on my own interests or observations, I want to identify problems based on what people are actively asking for and build tools that address them.

The objective is to develop an iterative process: discover a problem, build a solution, distribute it, learn from the experience, and move on to the next opportunity.

The Plan

Exploiting opportunities in the technological landscape involves three main steps.

1. Identify a Worthwhile Problem

Rather than limiting myself to problems I personally encounter or care about, I want to systematically search the internet for problems that people are actively trying to solve.

This approach has an inherent risk: I may pursue problems in domains where I have no particular expertise or competitive advantage. My primary advantage will therefore be the discovery process itself. By systematically scanning online discussions and requests, I hope to identify opportunities early, before others recognize their potential.

The tool I am developing for this purpose is Blindspot.

An opportunity does not necessarily need to have direct economic value. Its value could also come from reputation, visibility, useful connections, or social impact.

2. Build a Solution

Once a worthwhile problem has been identified, the next step is to build a tool that solves it.

The implementation approach will depend on the scope and complexity of the problem. I intend to experiment with different AI integration patterns and development workflows, choosing the simplest approach that can deliver an effective solution.

The objective is not to build sophisticated systems for their own sake, but to maximize the value delivered relative to the time and resources invested.

3. Publish and Distribute

Building a solution is only part of the process. A tool has little value if the people who need it never discover it.

Distribution will involve several complementary channels:

  • GitHub: Publish the tools as open-source projects.
  • Personal website: Document each project, explain its motivation, and share the reasoning behind its development.
  • Project-sharing platforms: Publish projects on websites where people discover and discuss new tools.
  • Direct engagement: Return to the discussions and communities where the original problems were identified, and offer the solution to people who expressed interest.

The last point is particularly important. The first step already produces a list of people who have expressed a need for a solution. Rather than relying entirely on passive discovery, I can return to those discussions and introduce the tool directly.

This creates a natural connection between problem discovery and distribution.
