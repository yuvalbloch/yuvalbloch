🚀 Beyond the Big Picture: Modeling Small-Scale Land Use to Mitigate Health Risks

A novel framework combining fractal geometry and CA to predict tick exposure risk in dynamic smallholder systems.

💡 The TL;DR (30-Second Summary)

Current land-use models often ignore the dynamics of smallholder farming and shifting cultivation, which involves rapid changes at tiny spatial scales. We developed a new framework using a fractal approach combined with cellular automata (CA) to accurately model these small-scale systems. Applying this to Madagascar, we found that simple policy changes—like aggregating small farms—can effectively mitigate future increases in human-tick exposure.

Core Results At a Glance:

  • Policy Impact: Potential increases in tick exposure linked to forest transitions can be significantly mitigated by aggregating agroforestry plots near villages.
  • Small-Scale Focus: We successfully model systems dominated by smallholder farming and shifting cultivation, which are major drivers of global land use change but are often overlooked.
  • Predictive Power: Our framework demonstrates the critical influence of higher-order landscape parameters (like fragmentation or fractal dimension) on ecological and health outcomes.

🧠 The Problem & Our Solution

The Challenge (The “Why”)

Existing models simplify land-use change by focusing on large, long-term transitions. They fail to capture the complexity of smallholder decisions and the rapid, cyclical transitions (shifting cultivation) that define the livelihoods of hundreds of millions. These small-scale dynamics are critical drivers of health risks like zoonotic disease emergence.

Our Fractal + CA Solution

We address this complexity by introducing a two-step framework:

  1. Prioritize Influence: We first identify the higher-order landscape parameters (e.g., measuring the degree of plot shape complexity using fractals) that most strongly drive the ecological impact (tick exposure).
  2. Focused Modeling: We then use Cellular Automata (CA) to accurately project future land-use maps, specifically focusing on capturing the change in those most influential parameters.

This approach offers a powerful alternative to trying to predict every individual small-scale land change.

🌎 Application: Human-Tick Exposure in Madagascar

We applied the framework to the SAVA region of northern Madagascar to predict the impact of future land use on human-tick exposure, a significant public health concern. The results provide direct, actionable insights for regional policy decisions.

  • Target Region: SAVA region, Northern Madagascar.
  • Health Outcome: Predicted impact on human-tick exposure.
  • Policy Implication: The model strongly supports policies that encourage land aggregation and sustainable agroforestry practices.

why it importent to predict land use in small holder farms in the tropics

Land use represents the fundamental interface between humanity and nature. Every human activity, from the urban centers of New York to the remote tribes of the Amazon, involves occupying and altering the environment to meet specific needs. This environment, in turn, critically impacts human well-being: our health, exposure to disease, and vulnerability to natural disasters are intrinsically linked to our surroundings and how we modify them. Land use change is a primary driver of global environmental crises, it the main cause of 85% of endangered species cases (Hald-Mortensen, 2023), the emergence of 27% of vector-borne zoonotic diseases (VBZD) (Swei, 2020), it is also the leading cause of soil loss (Borrelli, 2017), and accounts for almost a quarter of greenhouse gases (Smith, 2014). Predicting how different patterns of land-use change will unfold and affect these processes is crucial for policymakers and stakeholders to develop strategies that minimize negative consequences for both the environment and human populations.
